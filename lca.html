Plain-English Guide

What is Life Cycle Assessment?

Life cycle assessment (LCA) is a method for measuring the environmental impact of a product or process — from raw materials through manufacturing, use, and end of life. Instead of a vague sense that something is "green," an LCA gives you numbers, like kilograms of CO₂ per kWh of battery produced, that you can compare, improve, and defend.

The idea in one minute

Think of an LCA as a nutrition label for your process. Everything that goes in (materials, energy, water) and everything that comes out (emissions, waste) gets counted for each step — mixing, coating, assembly, recycling, whatever your process involves. Those inputs and outputs are then translated into a handful of environmental impact scores using standardized conversion factors.

The result: instead of "cobalt-free sounds cleaner," you can say "our cathode route emits 18% less CO₂ per kWh, but uses more freshwater — here's the breakdown by process step."

What an LCA actually measures

LiTeCAB scores processes on the nine impact categories defined by the US EPA's TRACI 2.1 method. In plain terms:

Impact categoryWhat it tells youTypical unit
Climate changeContribution to global warming — the "carbon footprint"kg CO₂-eq
AcidificationEmissions that cause acid rain and acidify soil and waterkg SO₂-eq
EutrophicationNutrient pollution that causes algae blooms and dead zones in waterkg N-eq
Smog formationEmissions that create ground-level ozone (smog)kg O₃-eq
Particulate matterFine-particle air pollution linked to respiratory healthkg PM2.5-eq
Ozone depletionDamage to the protective stratospheric ozone layerkg CFC-11-eq
Human toxicity (cancer)Potential carcinogenic effects on peopleCTUh
Human toxicity (non-cancer)Other toxic effects on peopleCTUh
Freshwater ecotoxicityToxic effects on aquatic lifeCTUe

The "-eq" in the units means "equivalents": different substances are converted to a common reference so they can be added up. For example, one kilogram of methane counts as roughly 28–36 kg CO₂-eq, because it traps that much more heat.

When do you need an LCA?

  • Funding proposals. ARPA-E, DOE, and EU programs increasingly ask applicants to quantify expected environmental benefits. A proposal with real per-kWh numbers reads very differently from one with adjectives.
  • Sustainability claims and PR. "Our process cuts emissions by a third" only survives scrutiny if there's an analysis behind it. An LCA is that analysis — and the guard against accidental greenwashing.
  • Beating the status quo. New processes get compared to the incumbent. An LCA lets you show, category by category, where yours wins — and where it doesn't yet.
  • Customer and regulatory requirements. Battery supply chains face growing disclosure rules (like the EU battery passport), and OEM customers pass those requirements upstream.
  • R&D screening. The cheapest time to fix an environmental hotspot is before scale-up. Early screening shows which process step drives impact so you work on the right problem.

Common questions

What's the difference between an LCA and a carbon footprint?

A carbon footprint is one slice of an LCA: it counts only greenhouse gas emissions. A full LCA also covers water pollution, air quality, toxicity, ozone depletion, and more — so a process that looks good on carbon alone can't hide problems elsewhere.

What is TRACI?

TRACI is the US EPA's method for turning a list of emissions into environmental impact scores. It defines the categories (climate change, acidification, smog, and so on) and the conversion factors used to score them. LiTeCAB uses TRACI version 2.1.

What data do I need to run an LCA?

At minimum: the materials and energy that go into each step of your process, in quantities per unit of product. This is called a life cycle inventory. In LiTeCAB, ready-made battery templates give you a complete starting inventory that you adjust to match your process.

How long does an LCA take?

A traditional consultant-led LCA typically takes weeks to months. A screening LCA in LiTeCAB — using templates and built-in data — takes minutes, which makes it practical to compare options during R&D rather than after decisions are made.

Is a screening LCA enough for a grant proposal or a published claim?

Screening results are well suited to proposals, internal decisions, and early comparisons. Public marketing claims or regulatory submissions usually call for a full ISO 14040/14044 study with reviewed data. A tailored LiTeCAB deployment on full datasets can be the foundation for that.
